**Decision status:** provisional assessment. Compare MIR-ATR and NIR reflection on client melts before selecting a process probe.

**Premise check:** The materials support this investigation: an extrusion application shows NIR monitoring and API spectra in hot polymer using diamond-cone ATR. This is not proof of performance for your formulation. [S02 p.14](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=14)

### Requirements
Stated by the client:
- API incorporation during hot polymer extrusion.
- Detection of composition changes during processing.
- Offline assays available.
- Hot interface with possible material buildup.

Unknown:
- Control decision, present losses, and required detection capability.
- API identity, polymer formulation, and composition ranges.
- Melt temperature, pressure, and optical appearance.
- Mounting location, cleaning access, and buildup behaviour.
- Assay uncertainty and sample-to-spectrum matching.

### Clarification questions
1. What failure, cost, and control decision motivate monitoring? Why it matters: if detecting excursions suffices, screening can prioritise discrimination rather than concentration prediction.
2. Which API, polymers, additives, and concentration ranges must be covered? Why it matters: if formulations change, the sample set and calibration must cover them.
3. What temperature and pressure ranges apply, and is the melt clear or scattering? Why it matters: if clear, test transmission instead of NIR reflection.
4. Where could a probe mount, and how does buildup develop and clear? Why it matters: if deposits persist, test cleaning or another sampling location.
5. What assay, uncertainty, minimum detectable change, and update interval are required? Why it matters: if assays cannot resolve that change, quantitative validation needs improvement.

### Measurement concept
Provisionally compare a melt-contact ATR tip with a NIR reflection window at the proposed mounting location.

The geometry table supports ATR for contact measurements and diffuse reflection for scattering media; it does not establish melt-specific probe ratings. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)

As an installation hypothesis, test a barrel or die-adaptor interface where the melt renews the measured surface. Select the fiber after identifying useful bands.

### Spectral range
API-specific bands remain unknown; screening must identify features distinguishable from polymer and additives.

Nominal NIR covers 780–2500 nm. [S01 p.10](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=10) Listed VIS-NIR silica ends at 2400 nm, so it cannot carry that entire nominal range. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

For MIR, compare selected bands with PIR or chalcogenide transmission before specifying the link. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

### Risks and uncertainty
In general, these mechanisms could defeat the concept:
- Deposits may replace fresh melt in the measured layer.
- Poor ATR contact or bubbles may interrupt the signal.
- Temperature and scattering changes may mimic composition changes.
- Assay uncertainty and sampling mismatch may obscure real changes.
- AFS must confirm thermal, pressure, chemical, cleaning, and optical compatibility before installation.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: measure representative client melts using both candidates. Span composition, temperature, physical variation, and clean versus fouled interfaces.
2. Reference comparison: assay the same samples independently. Validate single-method models on held-out batches before considering fusion; assess reference uncertainty.
3. In-line trial: test the selected interface under actual flow, buildup, and cleaning conditions. Match spectra with timed reference samples.
4. Scale-up: integrate the validated output and define model monitoring, formulation coverage, and recalibration triggers.

**Decision basis:** Agree go/no-go criteria for detectable change, validation error, update interval, and usable operation between cleaning events.
