**Decision status:** provisional assessment. Test NIR diffuse reflection first, comparing MIR-ATR on matched samples before selecting an in-line configuration.

**Premise check:** The materials support NIR reflection for opaque, concentrated dairy products and fat/protein modelling, but do not establish performance for your formulation. [S01 p.17](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=17)

### Requirements
Stated by the client:
- Opaque, concentrated milk flowing through a processing line.
- Earlier warning of fat and protein changes.
- Variable temperature and texture; laboratory checks retained.

Unknown:

### Clarification questions
1. What goes wrong before laboratory results arrive, what does it cost, and what action would earlier results trigger? Why it matters: if results trigger diversion, validation must cover transition material.
2. What fat/protein ranges, acceptable errors and warning interval do you require? Why it matters: if changes are smaller than reference uncertainty, that comparison cannot establish success.
3. What temperature range, texture changes and formulation variants occur? Why it matters: if these vary independently of composition, the sample set must separate their effects.
4. Where could a probe access flowing product, and what pressure and cleaning conditions apply? Why it matters: if product deposits there, another location or cleaning arrangement needs testing.
5. Which laboratory methods do you use, with what uncertainty and sampling procedure? Why it matters: if samples cannot be matched to spectra, apparent prediction error includes sampling mismatch.

### Recommendation

| Method | Why test it | Check first |
|---|---|---|
| NIR diffuse reflection | Published dairy application concept uses backscattered light to model fat and protein. [S01 p.17](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=17) | Separate composition from texture and temperature effects. |
| MIR-ATR | Molecular absorption offers a comparator using a contact interface suitable for liquids and pastes. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6) | Stable contact and representative surface composition. |

Start with separate models. Combine methods only if held-out validation demonstrates useful improvement over the best single method.

### Measurement concept
Test a flush-mounted NIR reflection window at a continuously wetted pipe location; this geometry appears in the dairy application concept. [S01 p.17](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=17)

Compare an immersed ATR crystal on matched laboratory samples. Select silica for NIR and an appropriate infrared fiber for ATR after identifying useful bands. Fiber families have different transmission windows. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

Mounting and cable routing remain provisional.

### Spectral range
NIR screening targets overlapping overtone and combination bands associated with molecular groups, including O–H, N–H and C–H; quantitative interpretation requires chemometrics. [S01 p.10](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=10)

The nominal NIR range is 780–2500 nm. [S01 p.10](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=10) VIS-NIR silica ends at 2400 nm, so full nominal coverage cannot be assumed. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

Select useful milk MIR bands during screening rather than assigning unverified bands now.

### Risks and uncertainty
- Reflection responds to surface properties and fat-globule size, not composition alone. [S01 p.17](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=17)
- ATR measures a thin contact layer rather than bulk product. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)
- In general, temperature, texture, fouling and bubbles can alter spectra independently of fat or protein.
- Calibration must cover your formulations and conditions; laboratory uncertainty limits quantitative validation.
- AFS must confirm component coverage, wetted-material compatibility, cleaning suitability and installation ratings.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: Measure representative client samples by both methods. Include composition extremes and independently varied temperature and texture where practicable.
2. Reference comparison: Retain laboratory assays on the same samples. Validate separate models on held-out batches, then test fusion only if needed.
3. In-line trial: Install the selected geometry. Compare synchronized spectra and laboratory samples through production changes and cleaning cycles.
4. Scale-up: Integrate results with the agreed production action. Establish laboratory surveillance, out-of-model alarms and recalibration after formulation or process changes.

**Decision basis:** Proceed only if held-out and in-line results meet client-agreed error, warning interval and robustness requirements.
