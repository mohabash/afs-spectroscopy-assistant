**Decision status:** provisional assessment. Test Raman first against paired glucose and lactate assays, with NIR as a single-method comparator.

**Premise check:** The materials support testing in situ metabolite spectroscopy in aqueous bioprocesses, but do not establish performance in your changing culture. [S01 p.16](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=16)

### Requirements
Stated by the client:
- Aqueous cell culture; glucose and lactate measured through sampling.
- Sampling delays obscure changes.
- Medium and cell population change during each run.
- Existing laboratory assays available for comparison; probe and calibration undecided.

Unknown:
- Measurement-driven action, acceptable prediction error and update interval.
- Glucose and lactate concentration ranges.
- Formulation, cell-density and turbidity changes.
- Reactor interface, cleaning conditions and assay uncertainty.

### Clarification questions
1. What action should the pilot support, with what error and update interval? Why it matters: if results drive feeding, validation must cover feeding transitions.
2. What glucose and lactate ranges occur? Why it matters: if low concentrations dominate, insufficient signal could eliminate a candidate.
3. Which feeds, formulations and cell-density changes occur? Why it matters: if these vary independently, the calibration samples must separate their effects.
4. Where can a probe enter, and what temperature, pressure and sterilization conditions apply? Why it matters: if sterilization occurs in place, probe compatibility becomes mandatory.
5. Which assays are used, with what uncertainty and sampling timestamps? Why it matters: if sample timing is uncertain, apparent prediction errors may reflect process changes.

### Recommendation

| Method | Why test it | Check first |
|---|---|---|
| Raman—preferred | The bioprocess overview identifies glucose and lactate as molecular targets in aqueous media. [S01 p.16](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=16) | Weak Raman signals and fluorescence background. [S01 p.11](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=11) |
| NIR—comparator | The bioprocess overview lists glucose and lactate as accessible variables. [S01 Fig.7 (p.17)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=17) | Overlapping bands require chemometrics. [S01 p.10](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=10) |

These overview examples justify screening, not culture-specific performance claims. Select a single method unless combining methods improves held-out predictions.

### Measurement concept
Use an immersion Raman probe at a representative reactor location, to be confirmed during installation review. Raman probes suit liquid samples. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)

For NIR, screen transmission or transflection while sufficient light passes; compare diffuse reflection if scattering dominates. These geometries address different sample optics. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)

Check the complete fiber–probe–spectrometer optical path before specifying the link.

### Spectral range
Screen Raman with laser excitation at 785 nm, a configuration described in the pharmaceutical process example—not validated here for cell culture. [S01 p.15](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=15) Establish useful Raman shifts in cm⁻¹ from your samples.

NIR’s nominal range is 780–2500 nm. [S01 p.10](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=10) Listed VIS-NIR silica ends at 2400 nm; full nominal coverage therefore needs another fiber configuration. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

### Risks and uncertainty
In general, the following mechanisms need testing:
- Changing cells and medium may alter scattering or fluorescence independently of metabolite concentrations.
- Glucose and lactate trends may correlate, allowing a model to learn run progression rather than distinguish analytes.
- Bubbles or deposits may change the material seen by the probe.
- Assay uncertainty and sampling mismatch limit demonstrated prediction quality.
- AFS must confirm component coverage, wetted materials, sterilization compatibility and installation.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: measure both candidates on client samples spanning metabolite ranges, culture stages, feeds and physical variation.
2. Reference comparison: obtain independent assays on the same samples. Validate separate analyte models on held-out runs before testing data fusion.
3. In-line trial: install the selected interface and collect synchronized spectra, samples and process records through changing culture conditions.
4. Scale-up: define integration, out-of-calibration warnings, model maintenance and revalidation after formulation or process changes.

**Decision basis:** Proceed only if held-out and in-line results meet jointly agreed error, timeliness and robustness requirements.
