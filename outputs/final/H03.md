2. Which organism, medium and glucose range apply, and how do you define viable biomass? Why it matters: if viability means colony formation, validation must target that endpoint.
3. Which independent glucose and viability assays are available, with what uncertainty? Why it matters: if viability references are unavailable, a fluorescence–viability model cannot be validated.
4. What excitation sources, emission coverage and probe connections does your spectrometer provide? Why it matters: if relevant fluorescence lies outside its coverage, existing hardware cannot measure it.
5. What uncertainty, reporting interval, temperature, turbidity, aeration and cleaning conditions must the system tolerate? Why it matters: if bubbles dominate the signal, the interface needs redesign.

### Risks and uncertainty
In general, screening must challenge these mechanisms:
- Metabolic fluorescence can change without a corresponding change in viable biomass.
- Feed additions can create glucose correlations that fail during another process phase.
- Bubbles, scattering and probe deposits can change collected light.
- New media or process conditions can invalidate calibration.
- Reference uncertainty limits quantitative validation.

AFS must confirm instrument compatibility, optical coverage and process-interface suitability; no performance is established yet.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: measure representative client broth spanning glucose, viability and physical variation with each candidate method.
2. Reference comparison: assay the same samples independently. Validate single-method models on held-out fermentation runs; combine methods only for demonstrated benefit.
3. In-line trial: challenge the selected interface during aeration, feeding and fouling under real process conditions.
4. Scale-up: integrate reporting, out-of-model warnings, reference checks and recalibration procedures.

**Decision basis:** proceed only if held-out runs meet client-agreed uncertainty and reporting requirements for each explicitly defined target.
