### Clarification questions
1. What triggers rejection, what does an event cost, and what action should monitoring drive? Why it matters: if rejection reflects appearance, screen optical changes before composition models.
2. What ingredients, formulation changes, and possible contamination sources occur? Why it matters: if normal formulations differ, the sample set must distinguish those differences from failures.
3. Is the liquid clear, turbid, multiphase, or bubble-containing during failures? Why it matters: if transmission loses its signal, test ATR or reflection geometry.
4. What process temperatures, pressures, cleaning chemicals, access points, and analyzer distance apply? Why it matters: if deposits cover the interface, installation and cleaning need testing.
5. Can you supply matched conforming and rejected samples, event records, independent assays, and acceptance requirements? Why it matters: if failure labels lack independent confirmation, screening establishes differences, not contamination detection.

In general, contamination remains a hypothesis: normal composition, phase changes, or process conditions could also explain spectral differences.

### Measurement concept
Begin with immersed ATR and a defined-gap transmission/transflection interface in the laboratory. Select the gap experimentally rather than prescribing it now.

Choose the process location after tracing the failure event. Fiber, crystal, seals, instrument coupling, and installation require AFS confirmation.

### Spectral range
No application-specific bands are established. Laboratory screening can explore nominal MIR at 4000–400 cm⁻¹, without assuming complete probe coverage. [S01 p.9](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=9)

Nominal NIR spans 780–2500 nm. [S01 p.10](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=10) However, the listed VIS-NIR silica family ends at 2400 nm; it cannot provide that entire nominal range. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

### Risks and uncertainty
- In general, a spectral anomaly does not establish contaminant identity or causation.
- ATR samples only a thin contact layer, not necessarily representative bulk liquid. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)
- Temperature, scattering, phase separation, or fouling could mimic composition changes.
- A model may learn batch differences instead of the failure mechanism.
- Quantitative claims require independent same-sample reference results; their uncertainty constrains validation.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: compare representative conforming and rejected samples with both candidates. Include normal formulation and physical variation; preserve event records.
2. Reference comparison: independently characterize the same samples using assays chosen after reviewing likely failure mechanisms. Validate single-method models on held-out batches before considering combinations.
3. In-line trial: test the selected interface against actual failure events, bubbles, deposits, and process variation.
4. Scale-up: integrate the validated measurement, define out-of-model warnings, and establish reference checks and recalibration triggers.

**Decision basis:** Proceed only if held-out results support the agreed process decision; otherwise investigate the failure mechanism before specifying a sensor.
