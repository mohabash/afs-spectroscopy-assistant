**Decision status:** provisional assessment. Do not approve the concept as specified; first test petroleum samples with MIR ATR and a compatible infrared fiber link.

**Premise check:** MIR ATR merits testing, but the proposed silica cable cannot carry the required MIR wavelengths; its documented transmission ends in the NIR. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

### Requirements
Stated by the client:
- Water content in a petroleum line, investigated using MIR ATR.
- Analyzer in another building.
- Reuse of an existing visible/NIR silica cable proposed.

Unknown:
- Operational decision, current losses, water range and reporting basis.
- Required uncertainty and update interval.
- Petroleum composition, water distribution and independent reference assay.
- Cable length, routing, process conditions and cleaning requirements.

### Clarification questions
1. What goes wrong today, what does it cost, and what action would the result trigger? Why it matters: if water excursions trigger diversion, screening must test excursion detection.
2. What water range, reporting basis, uncertainty and update interval are required? Why it matters: if the required changes are unresolved, ATR cannot support that decision.
3. Is water dissolved, emulsified or present as separate droplets, and which petroleum streams occur? Why it matters: if water separates, local ATR contact may not represent bulk content.
4. What are the link length, route, process temperature, pressure and cleaning conditions? Why it matters: if the remote link loses excessive signal, the analyzer location must change.
5. Which independent water assay and uncertainty are available? Why it matters: if reference uncertainty exceeds the required discrimination, quantitative validation cannot establish it.

### Recommendation
Test MIR ATR first. The AFS slide shows petroleum spectra labelled with different water contents, supporting a feasibility trial—not demonstrated performance for this line. [S02 p.13](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=13)

Use an infrared-compatible link rather than the existing silica cable. Chalcogenide fiber is a candidate because its documented transmission includes the illustrated water-band region. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

### Measurement concept
Screen an immersion ATR probe contacting representative petroleum samples. ATR geometry is listed for liquids and measures only a thin contact layer. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)

As an engineering proposal, install the selected probe where flowing petroleum continually wets the crystal. AFS must confirm the crystal, seals, mounting, instrument coupling and routed-link signal budget. If remote transmission fails, test an analyzer closer to the line.

### Spectral range
The petroleum example shows a water-dependent absorption region around 4000–3000 cm⁻¹. [S02 p.13](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=13)

Using those endpoints, wavelength = 10 000/wavenumber gives 2.5–approximately 3.33 µm. The documented VIS-NIR silica range ends at 2400 nm, below this region; chalcogenide fiber spans 1100–6500 nm. These are fiber-family ranges, not assembled-system specifications. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

### Risks and uncertainty
- In general, separated droplets can make local water content differ from the stream average.
- Fouling or bubbles can replace petroleum at the ATR contact surface.
- Temperature and petroleum-composition changes can mimic water changes; calibration must cover the intended streams and conditions.
- A compatible fiber family does not establish adequate signal through the unspecified route.
- Reference uncertainty limits the quantitative conclusion; no accuracy or detection limit is established here.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: measure client petroleum samples spanning water content, stream composition and physical variation. Test contact stability and water-dependent MIR features.
2. Reference comparison: assay the same samples independently. Build an ATR-only model and validate on held-out batches, including changed process conditions.
3. In-line trial: test the selected probe and actual routed link under operating conditions, including fouling, bubbles and cleaning.
4. Scale-up: confirm integration, reference checks, model maintenance and recalibration for new streams.

**Decision basis:** approve only if held-out validation and the installed-link trial meet client-agreed measurement and operational requirements.
