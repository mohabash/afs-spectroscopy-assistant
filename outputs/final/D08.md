**Decision status:** provisional assessment. Test a shared-tip MIR ATR–fluorescence probe against separate probes, then compare single-method and fused classification results.

**Premise check:** The materials support this laboratory comparison: a combined probe was demonstrated on chicken breast, fat and liver, using simultaneous measurements. [S04 p.3](/materials/S04_Combi_MIR_Fluorescence_2021.pdf#page=3)

### Requirements
Stated by the client:
- Laboratory classification of heterogeneous model tissues.
- Compare MIR ATR and fluorescence while reducing location mismatch.

Unknown:
- Whether interface evaluation or prediction of tissue identity is the primary endpoint.
- Sample preparation, independent specimen availability and class-label verification.
- Required classification performance and available excitation source.

### Clarification questions
1. Is the primary decision whether to adopt the combined interface or improve tissue classification? Why it matters: if interface performance dominates, matched single-probe comparisons become essential.
2. Will samples be fresh, frozen/thawed or otherwise treated? Why it matters: if preparation varies, each preparation must enter the sample set and validation.
3. Can independent specimens be reserved for validation? Why it matters: if only repeated spots are available, results cannot establish prediction on unseen specimens.
4. Which fluorescence excitation source is available, and what performance would justify adoption? Why it matters: if excitation differs, rescreen fluorescence features before fixing acceptance criteria.

### Recommendation
In this experimental design, expert reasoning favors testing each method alone before claiming a benefit from fusion.

| Method | Why test it | Check first |
|---|---|---|
| MIR ATR | Lipid-rich fat showed a discriminating carbonyl band. [S04 p.3](/materials/S04_Combi_MIR_Fluorescence_2021.pdf#page=3) | Crystal contact and tissue heterogeneity |
| Fluorescence | Liver showed a selective emission feature at 613 nm. [S04 p.3](/materials/S04_Combi_MIR_Fluorescence_2021.pdf#page=3) | Excitation, background and reproducibility |
| Combined | Fused data separated the model tissue classes in exploratory PCA. [S04 p.4](/materials/S04_Combi_MIR_Fluorescence_2021.pdf#page=4) | Improvement over the best single method |

### Measurement concept
Use the demonstrated truncated-cone ZrO₂ interface with separate CIR and silica channels for simultaneous ATR and fluorescence. [S04 p.2](/materials/S04_Combi_MIR_Fluorescence_2021.pdf#page=2)

For this proposed comparison, mark tissue locations and measure separate probes sequentially there. Repeat combined-probe measurements after repositioning. Keep preparation and contact procedures consistent.

### Spectral range
Prioritize MIR absorbance at 1743 cm⁻¹ and fluorescence emission at 613 nm as published comparison variables, not predetermined optimal features. [S04 p.5](/materials/S04_Combi_MIR_Fluorescence_2021.pdf#page=5)

The conversion is 10 000 / 1743 = 5.74 µm. This lies within the listed CIR transmission range, 1100–6500 nm. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

Excitation wavelength remains to be selected; emission wavelength does not specify excitation.

### Risks and uncertainty
- The channels share a tip but not identical sampled areas or depths. Local homogeneity remains relevant. [S04 p.2](/materials/S04_Combi_MIR_Fluorescence_2021.pdf#page=2)
- In general, variable pressure, incomplete contact or surface liquid could change ATR spectra without changing tissue identity.
- In general, repeated spots from one specimen can inflate apparent validation performance if split between training and testing.
- AFS must confirm excitation, component throughput, contact procedure and fiber routing before testing.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: use representative client tissues. The published template used three specimens per class and five distributed locations per specimen. [S04 p.3](/materials/S04_Combi_MIR_Fluorescence_2021.pdf#page=3)
2. Reference comparison: verify tissue labels independently on corresponding samples. Fit single-method models first, then fusion; reserve entire specimens for validation.
3. Interface trial: instead of an in-line trial, repeat laboratory measurements with controlled repositioning and contact changes. Compare combined and separate-probe signals.
4. Scale-up: retain laboratory scope. Extend specimen diversity and define calibration maintenance before considering another application.

**Decision basis:** Agree acceptance criteria beforehand; held-out results could demonstrate laboratory classification and interface utility, not clinical diagnosis or process readiness.
