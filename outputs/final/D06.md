**Decision status:** provisional assessment. Compare MIR-ATR and NIR on your petroleum samples, testing whether each tracks laboratory water results despite changes in dispersion.

**Premise check:** The materials support testing fiber-based ATR for petroleum water measurement, but the illustrated application does not establish your stream’s performance or remote-link feasibility. [S02 p.13](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=13)

### Requirements
Stated by the client:
- Petroleum stream; laboratory water determination.
- Earlier notice of changes; analyzer outside the process area.
- Comparison before equipment selection.

Unknown:
- Required warning time, water range, acceptable error, and consequences of missed changes.
- Petroleum grades, water state, turbidity, and process conditions.
- Reference assay uncertainty, installation interface, and cable route.

### Clarification questions
1. What action follows a water increase, what does delayed detection cost, and what warning time and error are acceptable? Why it matters: if only an alarm is needed, trend detection may suffice.
2. What water range occurs, and is water dissolved, emulsified, or present as droplets? Why it matters: if droplets separate, the sample set must test dispersion and representativeness.
3. Which petroleum grades, additives, temperatures, and turbidity changes occur? Why it matters: if these vary independently, calibration samples must separate their effects from water.
4. What laboratory assay, uncertainty, and sampling procedure are used? Why it matters: if sampling changes water distribution, spectral and reference results may disagree.
5. What process port, pressure, cleaning conditions, area classification, and routed analyzer distance apply? Why it matters: if the interface or cable route is unsuitable, the installation concept changes.

### Recommendation

| Method | Why test it | Check first |
|---|---|---|
| MIR-ATR | Petroleum-water spectra are illustrated; this is an application example, not validated performance for your stream. [S02 p.13](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=13) | Whether crystal contact represents bulk water. |
| NIR | O–H-related absorption provides a candidate signal; overlapping bands require chemometrics. [S01 p.10](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=10) | Whether petroleum absorption and scattering obscure water changes. |

Start with separate methods. Combine them only if held-out validation shows useful improvement over the best single method.

### Measurement concept
Test an immersed ATR crystal against NIR transmission or transflection for sufficiently transmitting samples. Diffuse reflection is a conditional alternative for scattering samples. These geometries match the sample categories in the comparison table. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)

Provisionally place the probe in representative flowing material. AFS must confirm the interface, component compatibility, fiber throughput, and installation suitability before selecting the external analyzer.

### Spectral range
The petroleum example shows water-related absorption roughly within 4000–3000 cm⁻¹. [S02 p.13](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=13)

Conversion: 10 000/4000 = 2.5 µm; 10 000/3000 ≈ 3.33 µm. CIR coverage of 1100–6500 nm encompasses this interval; PIR starts at 3000 nm and misses its shorter-wavelength portion. Do not assume PIR covers the complete illustrated band. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

Select useful NIR wavelengths from screening, not from the nominal method range.

### Risks and uncertainty
- ATR samples a thin contact layer rather than the bulk. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6) In general, droplets, bubbles, or fouling could dominate that layer.
- In general, temperature, petroleum composition, and scattering could mimic water changes; test them independently.
- A quantitative model needs matched independent references; their uncertainty limits achievable agreement.
- No transferable calibration or remote-link performance has been established for this stream.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: measure both candidates on client samples spanning water content, petroleum grades, temperature, and dispersion changes. Test repeatability after remixing and settling.
2. Reference comparison: obtain laboratory results on matched samples. Build single-method models first; validate on held-out batches, including independent physical variation.
3. In-line trial: test the selected geometry and intended fiber route. Compare warnings with matched laboratory samples and inspect for fouling or phase separation.
4. Scale-up: confirm integration, cleaning, reference checks, model applicability limits, and recalibration triggers.

**Decision basis:** Proceed only if agreed warning, error, robustness, and installation requirements are met on independent samples and during the process trial.
