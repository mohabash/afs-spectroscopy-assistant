**Decision status:** provisional assessment. Test NIR transmission/transflection on clear and turbid whey, with diffuse reflection as the fallback if transmission loses usable signal.

**Premise check:** The materials support NIR transmission for relatively clear whey, but not an assumption of constant clarity; increasing opacity can defeat transmission. [S01 p.18](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=18)

### Requirements
Stated by the client:
- Relatively clear whey before concentration.
- Protein concentration tracking against laboratory results.
- Turbidity increases under some operating conditions.

Unknown:
- The process decision, current losses, and required measurement interval.
- Protein definition, concentration range, and acceptable prediction error.
- Turbidity and temperature ranges, laboratory assay uncertainty, and installation constraints.

### Clarification questions
1. What goes wrong today, what does it cost, and what action would protein results trigger? Why it matters: if results control diversion, validation must cover that decision boundary.
2. What protein definition, concentration range, prediction error, and update interval are required? Why it matters: if changes approach reference uncertainty, quantitative tracking may not be demonstrable.
3. Which conditions increase turbidity, and how do temperature and composition change? Why it matters: if turbidity changes independently of protein, calibration samples must separate those effects.
4. Which laboratory assay is used, with what uncertainty and sample handling? Why it matters: if it measures total nitrogen rather than the intended protein fraction, the calibration target needs clarification.
5. Which process ports, pressures, cleaning conditions, and cable routes are available? Why it matters: if the optical gap traps deposits, the interface or installation location must change.

### Recommendation
Use NIR as the first method, comparing alternative geometries rather than combining spectral methods initially.

| Method | Why test it | Check first |
|---|---|---|
| NIR transmission/transflection | Materials identify this geometry for relatively clear whey. [S01 p.18](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=18) | Whether useful transmission survives the turbid conditions. |
| NIR diffuse reflection | Suitable for scattering media. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6) | Whether backscatter remains useful when whey clears. |

These are application guidance, not demonstrated performance for this stream.

### Measurement concept
Screen adjustable transmission/transflection gaps on flowing client whey. Compare diffuse reflection at the same process location and matched times.

As an engineering hypothesis, prefer a single transflection interface if it works across both clarity states. Otherwise, compare reflection alone before considering separate optical channels. The materials acknowledge transflection can sometimes span differing dairy optical states. [S01 p.18](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=18)

Use a VIS-NIR silica fiber link; installation and required cable length need AFS confirmation.

### Spectral range
Screen broad NIR features rather than selecting protein wavelengths prematurely. The nominal NIR region is 780–2500 nm; overlapping molecular bands require chemometric analysis. [S01 p.10](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=10)

The listed VIS-NIR silica family transmits 350–2400 nm, so it does not support that entire nominal region. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6) Confirm actual instrument and probe coverage before screening.

### Risks and uncertainty
- Transmission may saturate as whey becomes opaque. [S01 p.18](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=18)
- In general, scattering, temperature, bubbles, or deposits can mimic concentration changes; correlated constituents can also produce misleading protein predictions.
- A model for this whey cannot be assumed valid for another formulation or concentrated stream.
- Reference uncertainty limits quantitative validation.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: measure representative whey spanning protein concentration, clarity, temperature, and operating states with both geometries.
2. Reference comparison: assay the same samples independently. Validate single-geometry models on held-out production runs, including clarity transitions.
3. In-line trial: test the selected interface before concentration under real flow, fouling, and cleaning conditions.
4. Scale-up: integrate results, define model validity checks, and establish reference sampling and recalibration procedures.

**Decision basis:** Proceed only if held-out and in-line results meet client-agreed error, availability, and maintenance requirements across both clarity states.
