### Clarification questions
1. What goes wrong today, what does it cost, and what action would an earlier warning trigger? Why it matters: if intervention is possible, validation must test warning time before that intervention.
2. Does growth mean dry biomass, total cells, or viable cells? Why it matters: if viability matters, turbidity alone cannot establish the target.
3. Which metabolic change matters: substrate depletion, product accumulation, or cellular state? Why it matters: if a specific metabolite matters, fluorescence may not measure it directly.
4. Which strains, media, feeds, and operating conditions vary? Why it matters: if formulations change, screening and validation must include them.
5. Which reference assays, sampling access, and probe ports are available? Why it matters: if matched samples are unavailable, quantitative calibration cannot proceed.

### Recommendation
In general, start with the simplest biomass measurement, then assess whether a separate metabolic channel adds useful warning information.

| Method | Why test it | Check first |
|---|---|---|
| UV–Vis attenuation | Cell-suspension turbidity can track biomass-related changes. [S01 p.16](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=16) | Signal loss as broth becomes opaque. |
| Fluorescence | Defined cofactors, including NADH and riboflavin, offer candidate signals. [S01 p.16](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=16) | Which species and biological change the signal represents. |

### Measurement concept
Screen transmission/transflection for attenuation and an immersion fluorescence probe in representative broth; these interfaces are listed for liquid samples. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)

In general, place the selected probes where mixed broth reaches them consistently, with paired reference sampling nearby. Installation needs AFS confirmation.

Select the silica fiber family only after fixing excitation and detection wavelengths; check both against its transmission window. [S02 p.6](/materials/S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf#page=6)

### Spectral range
No application-specific wavelengths are justified yet. Screen attenuation across the available UV–Vis region and fluorescence excitation/emission regions appropriate to the selected species. Fluorescence requires a fluorescent component, not merely a desired analyte. [S01 p.5](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=5)

### Risks and uncertainty
In general, screening must challenge these mechanisms:
- Transmission may saturate as broth opacity rises. [S01 p.16](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=16)
- Cell morphology, bubbles, or medium colour may mimic biomass changes.
- Fluorescence may track biomass rather than provide independent metabolic information.
- ATR is not an automatic replacement for turbidity: it samples a thin contact layer, making fouling and bubbles important. [S01 Table 1 (p.6)](/materials/S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf#page=6)
- Reference uncertainty and strain or formulation changes may limit model transfer.

AFS must confirm component compatibility, cleaning suitability, and usable signal under process conditions.

### Feasibility plan
1. Lab screening: Measure client broth across growth stages, optical densities, and relevant deviations using both candidates. Preserve each sample’s process history.
2. Reference comparison: As proposed reference measurements, pair spectra with dry biomass, cell counts, and viability measurements where relevant. Add an independent assay for the chosen metabolite or cofactor. Test single-method models first; validate on held-out runs before evaluating combined signals.
3. In-line trial: Test the selected geometry through complete runs, including aeration, feeding, and cleaning. Compare warnings with matched references and recorded process events.
4. Scale-up: Integrate only validated outputs. Establish model checks and new reference collection after strain, medium, or process changes.

**Decision basis:** Proceed only if held-out runs meet client-agreed biomass performance and demonstrate useful, independently verified warning information.
