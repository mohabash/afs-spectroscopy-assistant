[Figure: Figure 2] The figure consists of two line plots labeled (a) and (b), showing mean spectra (solid lines) with shaded standard deviation intervals for chicken breast (blue), fat (red), and liver (brown). Plot (a) shows IR spectra with the horizontal axis labeled "wavenumber, cm⁻¹" running from 1800 down to 1500 (ticks at 1800, 1750, 1700, 1650, 1600, 1550, 1500) and the vertical axis labeled "normalized absorbance" from 0 to 1 (ticks at 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1). In (a), the red curve (fat) exhibits a sharp peak near 1740 cm⁻¹ (mean around 0.7, with shaded standard deviation reaching nearly 1.0), whereas the blue and brown curves have only minor shoulders near 0.2; all three curves show peaks around 1640 cm⁻¹ (blue and brown peaking at 1.0, red mean peaking around 0.8 with a wide shaded band from about 0.6 to 1.0) and around 1548 cm⁻¹ (all peaking near 0.6). Plot (b) shows fluorescence spectra with the horizontal axis labeled "wavelength, nm" from 500 to 750 (ticks at 500, 550, 600, 650, 700, 750) and the vertical axis labeled "normalized intensity" from 0 to 1 (ticks at 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1). In (b), all three curves rise sharply from 0 at 495 nm to a main maximum of 1.0 around 525–530 nm and then decay toward 0 near 780 nm; the brown curve (liver) displays a distinct secondary peak around 613 nm at a normalized intensity of about 0.4, while the blue and red curves decline smoothly through this region without a peak.
